VAT (in Dutch: **btw**) sounds complicated, but it is simple once you know the steps. You add up the VAT your clients paid. You subtract the VAT you paid yourself. The difference you pay — or get back.

Let's walk through it calmly.

## What is a VAT return?

VAT is a tax on goods and services. As a business you charge VAT to your clients (usually 21%). That VAT is not yours — you must pass it on to the tax office (**Belastingdienst**).

But you also pay VAT yourself, on your business costs. You can claim that back. This is called **input VAT** (*voorbelasting*).

Most freelancers file **every quarter** (every three months).

## Step 1 — Add up the VAT on your sales

Add up all the VAT you charged clients during the quarter. This is the VAT you **must pay**.

Example: you invoiced € 10,000 with 21% VAT. That is **€ 2,100 in VAT**.

## Step 2 — Add up your input VAT

Add up all the VAT you paid yourself on business costs: laptop, software, office supplies, and so on. This is the VAT you **get back**.

Example: you bought € 2,000 of goods with 21% VAT. That is **€ 420 input VAT**.

## Step 3 — Subtract and see the result

Now subtract the input VAT from the VAT on your sales:

| | Amount |
| --- | --- |
| VAT on your sales | € 2,100 |
| Less: input VAT | − € 420 |
| **To pay** | **€ 1,680** |

Is your input VAT higher than the VAT on your sales? Then you **get money back**. This often happens in a quiet quarter or when you made big purchases.

## Step 4 — File on time

You file online, through the Belastingdienst or through software. Do it **on time** — usually within one month after the quarter. Late means a fine.

> Tip: set the amount to pay aside as soon as you know it. Then there is never a surprise.

## The rates in short

- **21%** — the standard rate for most goods and services.
- **9%** — the low rate, for example food, books and some services.
- **0% or exempt** — for certain cases, such as exports or the KOR scheme.
