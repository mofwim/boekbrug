More and more you hear the term **e-invoice** or **UBL**. Your accountant asks for it, or a big client or the government wants to receive invoices only this way. But what exactly is it? And do you need it? Here's the simple version.

## What is an e-invoice (UBL)?

A normal PDF invoice is made to be read by **people**. An **e-invoice** is made to be read by **software**. **UBL** is the standard language that e-invoice is written in.

A UBL file holds all the details (supplier, amount, VAT, invoice number) in a fixed, structured way. That lets the recipient's accounting software load the invoice automatically — without retyping.

## Why would you use it?

- **Your accountant wants it.** UBL loads neatly into accounting software, so less manual work and fewer mistakes.
- **The government asks for it.** If you work for government bodies, e-invoicing is often required.
- **Big clients ask for it.** More and more companies process invoices automatically.

For many small freelancers a neat PDF is enough for now. But it helps to know you can also deliver UBL the moment someone asks.
