An invoice is not just a note with an amount. The Dutch tax office (**Belastingdienst**) has rules. If your invoice is wrong, your client sometimes cannot deduct the VAT — and that causes trouble.

Here is a simple checklist. Follow it and you are fine.

## What must be on your invoice?

- **The word "invoice"** (*factuur*) and the **invoice date**.
- **An invoice number.** Use consecutive numbers. Each number may exist only once.
- **Your details:** name, address, your **VAT number** (*btw-id*) and your **Chamber of Commerce number** (*KvK-nummer*).
- **Your client's details:** name and address.
- **What you delivered:** a short description of the product or service, and how much.
- **The date** you delivered the goods or did the service.
- **The amounts:** the amount without VAT, the **VAT rate** (21%, 9% or 0%), the VAT amount, and the total.

Selling to another business in the EU? Then also add your **client's VAT number**.

## An example of how the amounts look

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Amount without VAT | € 1,000.00 |
| VAT 21% | € 210.00 |
| **Total** | **€ 1,210.00** |

This shows your client exactly where the VAT is. That is required.

## Do you not charge VAT? (KOR or exempt)

Are you in the **small business scheme** (*kleineondernemersregeling*, KOR) or VAT-exempt? Then two things apply:

- You put **no VAT** on your invoice.
- You add a short line, for example: **"VAT-exempt under the KOR scheme"**.

Watch the limit: the KOR applies up to **€ 20,000 in revenue** per year. Go above it, and you must charge VAT again from that moment.

## Common mistakes

- **Skipping or repeating numbers.** Keep your numbering clean and consecutive.
- **Forgetting your VAT number.** Without it, the invoice is incomplete.
- **No delivery date.** The invoice date and the delivery date are not always the same.
