Every client base has its fixed group: the client who only moves after two e-mails and a phone call, then sends a zip with half the records, and is genuinely surprised something is missing. You can turn hard ("deliver by the 15th or I won't file your return") or turn resigned — but neither solves it, because next quarter it happens again.

## Why insisting doesn't work

The client who doesn't deliver is rarely unwilling. What's actually happening:

- **The receipt is already gone by the time you ask for it.** Between the fuel stop in March and your e-mail in July sit four months and one coat pocket. The request is structurally too late.
- **Delivery is a project for the client.** Make a folder, hunt receipts, export the bank, mail everything — that's an evening of work they keep postponing. Not because it's hard, but because it never feels urgent.
- **They don't know what "complete" means.** You see instantly that a month of bank statements is missing. They see a folder with a great many PDFs and think: that must be everything.

Asking more sternly changes none of these three causes. The moment, the effort and the overview have to change.

## What does work: three shifts

**1. Move capture to the moment itself.** A receipt photographed within a minute of the purchase never gets lost again. That requires an app on the client's phone where scanning is faster than pocketing the receipt — and where an invoice arriving by e-mail lands in the administration with a single forward. (The seven-year Dutch retention obligation is covered by that digital copy at the same time.)

**2. Make "what's still missing?" visible — to both of you.** As long as only you know what's missing, you remain the bottleneck *and* the bad guy. In BoekBrug the client sees per quarter what's still open, and you see the same thing on the werkboard: which client is complete and which still misses two things. Same facts, no argument.

**3. Remind specifically, not generally.** "Remember your administration" gets ignored. "2 things are still missing for Q3: the August bank statement and your phone subscription invoice" — that's something a person can act on, from their phone, in two minutes. In BoekBrug that message is one click from your werkboard.

## What you can do this week

1. Take your three most difficult deliverers — not your whole base.
2. Put them on a delivery app and walk along the first week (or point them at the short in-app course). You request the mandate from your own screen; the client approves it.
3. Measure one thing: how many reminders this quarter took, against the previous one.

If it works with those three, it works with the rest of the "never delivers" group — those clients resemble each other more than they resemble the rest of your base. And if a client does nothing even *with* a scan app, you've learned something else: it's not a convenience problem but a client problem, and that's a different conversation.
