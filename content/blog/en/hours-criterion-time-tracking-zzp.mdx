Want the **self-employed deduction**? Then you must meet the **hours criterion** (*urencriterium*). It sounds complicated, but it comes down to one number: **1,225 hours** per year. Here is what that means, in plain English, and how to track it.

## What is the hours criterion?

The hours criterion is the rule that you spend at least **1,225 hours** on your business in a calendar year. Meet it, and you qualify for the **self-employed deduction** (in 2026: € 1,200) and, as a starter, the **starter's deduction** (€ 2,123).

1,225 hours sounds like a lot, but it is about **24 hours a week** across the year. So you do not have to be a full-time entrepreneur.

## Which hours count?

Not only the hours you bill to a client. **All** hours for your business count — including the unpaid ones:

- work for clients (your billable hours);
- **acquisition**: writing quotes, networking, updating your website;
- **admin**: making invoices, processing receipts, your VAT return;
- **travel time** to clients;
- training and study for your profession.

These "indirect" hours are often forgotten, yet they add up quickly.

## Note: the SME profit exemption is separate

Important difference: you get the **SME profit exemption** (12.7%) **even without** the hours criterion. Only the self-employed deduction and starter's deduction depend on the 1,225 hours.

## Why you keep a time record

The tax office can ask you to **prove** you met the hours criterion. Without a record you cannot. So track, all year, how many hours you work and on what. It can be simple — a spreadsheet or an app — as long as it is **complete and credible**.

Tips:
- Log your hours **weekly**, not afterwards from memory.
- Also write down your **indirect** hours (admin, acquisition, travel).
- Keep the record for **7 years**, like the rest of your administration.
