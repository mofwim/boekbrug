As a freelancer you receive the whole amount from your client — including VAT and before tax. It feels like a lot of money, but part of it isn't yours. Set nothing aside and the tax bill is a shock. Set aside smartly and you never have stress.

Here's a simple guide to how much to save.

## What do you still have to pay?

Later, three things come off your revenue:

1. **VAT (btw)** — which you charge clients and pass on to the tax office.
2. **Income tax** — on your profit.
3. **Healthcare contribution (Zvw)** — a small contribution for healthcare.

The VAT isn't yours; you are only "passing it on". The tax and Zvw depend on your profit.

## Set the VAT aside immediately

The simplest step: don't count the VAT as your money. Receive an invoice of € 1,210 (€ 1,000 + € 210 VAT)? Put that € 210 aside right away. At your return you pay it (minus your input VAT).

## A rule of thumb for the tax

For income tax and Zvw together, many freelancers use this rule of thumb:

> Set aside **25% to 35% of your profit** for tax.

Earn little, and you're at the lower end. Earn more, and you move up, because you enter a higher bracket. It's a safe estimate — better a little too much than too little.

## Do it per month or per invoice

Don't wait until year-end. Two easy ways:

- **Per invoice:** every time you get paid, put a share aside straight away.
- **Per month:** at month-end you look at your profit and set aside a percentage.

Ideally use a **separate savings account**. Then you won't spend it by accident.
