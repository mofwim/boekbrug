Do you really have to keep every receipt? Yes. And longer than you think. The Dutch tax office (**Belastingdienst**) can ask to see your records later. If you can't show anything, you have a problem.

Luckily, it doesn't have to be a shoebox full of paper.

## How long must you keep records?

The basic rule is simple: you keep your records for **7 years**. That covers:

- **Invoices** you sent and received.
- **Receipts** for business purchases.
- Your **bank statements** and your VAT returns.

For records about **real estate** (like a business building) a longer period applies: **10 years**.

## Paper or digital?

Good news: you may keep everything **digitally**. A photo or scan of a receipt is enough, as long as it is clearly readable. You don't have to keep the paper afterwards.

That helps, because paper receipts fade. After a year in a drawer the ink is often gone. A photo stays readable forever.

## The biggest mistake: saving it all for the end

Many freelancers throw receipts in a folder and only deal with them at year-end. That costs a whole day of sorting — and some receipts are always missing. Every lost receipt is VAT you can't reclaim. That is a waste of money.

Better: **capture it right away**. A receipt at the till? Take a photo immediately. Done.
