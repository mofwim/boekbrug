Before you win a job, you often send a **quote** (*offerte*) first: a proposal of what you will do and what it costs. A clear, professional quote is the difference between "yes" and "we'll think about it". Here is how to write one.

## Quote or invoice — what's the difference?

People sometimes mix them up:

- A **quote** (*offerte*) is sent **beforehand**. It is a proposal: this is what I'll do, this is the price, this is how long the offer is valid.
- An **invoice** (*factuur*) is sent **afterwards**, once the work is done or delivered. That is the bill that must be paid.

So the order is: quote → agreement → do the work → invoice.

## What belongs on a quote?

Include at least:

- your **business details** (name, address, KVK number) and the client's details;
- a **clear description** of the work or delivery;
- the **price**, and whether it is **including or excluding VAT**;
- the **validity period** ("this quote is valid until ...");
- any **conditions**, the **payment term**, and the expected **delivery time**.

A quote is usually not legally required, but it prevents misunderstandings — which saves hassle later.

## Tips to make your quote win

- **Be concrete.** Break big jobs into parts with prices. That builds trust.
- **Offer choices.** A basic and an extended option helps the client choose "which", not "whether".
- **Add a validity period.** It gives a reason not to wait forever.
- **Follow up.** Heard nothing after a week? Send a friendly note. Many jobs come in after one reminder.
- **Keep it simple and tidy.** A clean layout signals professionalism.

## From quote to invoice

Once the client agrees and the work is done, turn your quote into an **invoice**. Make sure the invoice has the same description and price as the quote — then expectations match.
