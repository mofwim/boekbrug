Are you an expat in the Netherlands who wants to work for yourself? Good news: freelancing here is very doable, even if your Dutch isn't strong yet. This guide gives you the big picture in plain English, and links to deeper articles for each step.

A freelancer in the Netherlands is called a **ZZP'er** (*zelfstandige zonder personeel* — self-employed without staff).

## Step 1 — Register with the KvK

You start by registering with the **Chamber of Commerce** (*Kamer van Koophandel*, KvK). You get a KvK number, and shortly after, a VAT number (*btw-id*) from the tax office. You need to be a resident with a citizen service number (BSN) to register.

## Step 2 — Understand VAT (btw)

As a freelancer you usually charge **21% VAT** to clients and pass it on to the tax office. You can reclaim the VAT on your business costs. Most freelancers file a VAT return every quarter. See our [VAT guide](/en/blog/dutch-vat-guide) for the full picture.

## Step 3 — Send correct invoices

Your invoice must meet Dutch rules: your details, your VAT number, a unique number, and the VAT. You can invoice in English, but keep the required fields. See [what a Dutch invoice must include](/en/blog/dutch-invoice-requirements).

## Step 4 — Know your tax

You pay income tax on your profit, but you also get benefits like the self-employed deduction and the SME profit exemption. How much you keep is explained in the [freelancer tax guide](/en/blog/freelancer-tax-netherlands-2026).

## Step 5 — Keep your admin tidy

Keep your invoices, receipts and bank statements together, and track your VAT. This makes tax time painless. See the [bookkeeping guide](/en/blog/bookkeeping-guide-netherlands).

## A few expat-specific tips

- **You can work in English**, but official letters from the tax office come in Dutch. A translation tool or your accountant helps.
- **Check the 30% ruling** and other schemes with a tax adviser — expat situations can differ.
- **A Dutch bookkeeper** who is used to expats can save you a lot of stress.
