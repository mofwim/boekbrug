Do you work for a foreign client, or simply for an expat in the Netherlands? Then you might want to send your invoice in English. Good news: **you can**. You just need to watch a few things.

## An English invoice is fine

The tax office doesn't prescribe a language. So your invoice may be in English — as long as **all the required fields** are on it. Those don't change: your details with VAT number and Chamber of Commerce number, your client's details, a unique invoice number, the date, a description and the amounts with VAT. See the full list in [what a Dutch invoice must include](/en/blog/dutch-invoice-requirements).

## Watch the VAT with foreign clients

The language is the easy part; the **VAT** is where you must pay attention:

- **Client in the Netherlands (including an expat):** just Dutch VAT, usually 21%.
- **Business in the EU with a valid VAT number:** you often shift the VAT ("reverse charge"). Then put your client's VAT number on the invoice.
- **Client outside the EU:** often 0% VAT, but the rules differ per situation.

VAT rules for foreign clients are a topic of their own. In doubt? Ask your bookkeeper.

## Handy words on an English invoice

- Factuur → **Invoice**
- Factuurnummer → **Invoice number**
- Vervaldatum → **Due date**
- Bedrag zonder btw → **Amount excl. VAT**
- Btw → **VAT**
- Totaal → **Total**
- Btw verlegd → **VAT reverse-charged**
