Do you travel for business in your own car, bike or motorbike? Then you may charge a fixed amount per kilometre. In 2026 that is **€ 0.25 per kilometre**. This is called the tax-free mileage allowance.

Here it is, simply.

## What is the mileage allowance?

It is a fixed amount you may charge per business kilometre, without having to keep receipts for fuel or maintenance. The rate applies to most vehicles: car, motorbike and bike.

In 2026 the rate is **€ 0.25 per kilometre** (in 2025 it was € 0.23).

## How does it work for you as a freelancer?

There are two situations:

- **Your car is private.** Then for your business trips you may record € 0.25 per kilometre as a cost. That lowers your profit, and so your tax.
- **Your car is on the business.** Then it works differently: your costs are already business costs, and for private trips there is an addition (*bijtelling*). This article is about the first, most common situation.

## An example

Say you drive **3,000 business kilometres** in a year in your private car:

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Business kilometres | 3,000 |
| Rate 2026 | € 0.25 |
| **Deductible** | **€ 750** |

You take that € 750 off your profit. At an average tax rate that quickly saves a few hundred euros in tax.

## What counts as a business trip?

- To a client or a job.
- To a supplier or the bank.
- To a networking event or a course for your work.

Commuting to a fixed workplace usually doesn't count. Unsure? Ask your bookkeeper.

## Log your trips

You must be able to show how many business kilometres you drove. Keep a simple **trip log**: date, from–to, and the number of kilometres. Do this right after the trip, not from memory at year-end.
