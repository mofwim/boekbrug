What do you charge per hour? For many freelancers (**ZZP'ers**) this is a hard question. Charge too little and you keep nothing. Charge too much and you fear losing clients.

The good news: you can simply calculate your rate. You don't guess — you start from what you need.

## A rate is not a salary

A common mistake: comparing your hourly rate to an hourly wage in employment. That is wrong. As a freelancer you pay for:

- **Costs** (laptop, software, insurance, accountant).
- **Tax** on your profit.
- **Hours nobody pays for** (admin, finding clients, writing quotes).
- **No paid leave** — you pay for holidays and sick days yourself.
- **Pension** and a buffer for quiet months.

That is why a freelance rate is always higher than an hourly wage. It is not a luxury — it is necessary.

## Work it out in four steps

**Step 1 — What do you want to earn per year?**
Start with the amount you need to live on. For example € 40,000.

**Step 2 — Add your costs.**
Include your yearly business costs. For example € 8,000.

**Step 3 — How many hours do you really work for clients?**
A year has many hours, but not all are paid. Take out holidays, sick days, admin and finding clients. Often about **1,000 to 1,200 billable hours** remain.

**Step 4 — Divide and round.**
Divide your total (earnings + costs, plus a buffer for tax) by your billable hours. That gives you a rate per hour.

> Rule of thumb: don't use all 2,080 working hours of a full-time year. Use the hours clients actually pay for. That makes a big difference.

## Also look at the market

Your calculation gives you a **floor**: below this you earn too little. Then look at what is normal in your field and region. Well below average? Your rate can often go up.
