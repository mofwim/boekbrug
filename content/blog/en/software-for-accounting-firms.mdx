If you run an administratiekantoor in the Netherlands, you don't lack software. You have a bookkeeping package (Exact, SnelStart, Twinfield, Yuki — often several side by side), filing software, the Belastingdienst portal, and probably a drawer full of client credentials. Yet your margin doesn't evaporate in the bookkeeping itself. It evaporates **before the bookkeeping can start**.

## The real time sink isn't your bookkeeping package

Add up what an average quarter costs you in this kind of work:

- e-mailing or calling clients because the records still haven't arrived;
- receiving what does arrive as WhatsApp photos, folders of PDFs, or a shoebox;
- working out what's **missing** — which month of bank statements, which purchase invoice, which receipt;
- chasing that again;
- times fifty, a hundred or two hundred clients.

None of this appears on an invoice as a line item, but it decides how many clients one employee can carry. Bookkeeping software doesn't fix it, because bookkeeping software only starts working **once the administration already exists**.

## What "software for an accounting firm" actually has to do

So the question isn't "which package books most comfortably?" — you settled that long ago. The question is: **what makes every client deliver a complete administration, without you chasing them?** Concretely:

1. **Clients must barely be able to get delivery wrong.** Photograph the receipt, forward the invoice by e-mail, upload the bank export — it must not be more than that. Every extra step is a document that never arrives.
2. **You must be able to see, per client, what's missing.** Not in your head, not in a spreadsheet you maintain yourself — on one screen: client, quarter, what's in, what's not.
3. **Reminding must be one click.** A client missing two things gets a message naming exactly those two things. Not a bulk mail everyone ignores.
4. **The result must flow into your own package.** What the client delivers should reach you as an ordered package or an audit file (XAF) — not something you retype.

## How BoekBrug fills that in

BoekBrug is not a bookkeeping package for your firm and doesn't want to be. It's the **delivery layer** between your firm and your small-business clients: the client uses a simple app (scan receipts, create and forward invoices, connect the bank), and you watch from the firm side:

- The **werkboard** shows all your linked clients with the quarter's status: what's in, how many things are still missing, and for which client.
- **Requesting documents** and **sending a reminder** happen from that same screen — the message names what's missing.
- Access is arranged properly: you **request a mandate**, the client approves it. No shared passwords.
- Per quarter you download a **closing package** (invoices, receipts, bank, VAT substantiation), and per year an **XAF 3.2 audit file** with RGS references on the main accounts — your own package imports it directly. What the app can't place is named on a suspense account instead of guessed.
- You can invite an entire client base **in bulk**: paste a list of e-mail addresses, and see per address whether the invitation landed.

## Be honest about what software does *not* solve

Two things you shouldn't expect from any delivery layer, including BoekBrug:

- **The filing itself.** BoekBrug prepares the VAT figures and shows where they come from, but submitting to the Belastingdienst is yours, in your process.
- **Sending over Peppol.** Every invoice can be downloaded as a Peppol BIS 3.0 document, but transmission over the network requires an access-point contract — BoekBrug doesn't have that yet.

A vendor claiming "everything is automatic" is selling you a demo. The gain is elsewhere: when chasing, collecting and puzzling out fifty client administrations shrinks from days to a morning, you have room for more clients with the same people — or for advisory work that *does* appear on the invoice.

Pick one or two clients who are structurally late and try it with them for one quarter. If it works, you'll know for certain with exactly the clients where it hurts.
