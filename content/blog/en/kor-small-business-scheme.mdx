Do you have a small revenue? Then you can join the **small business scheme** (*kleineondernemersregeling*, KOR). You then charge no VAT and file no VAT return. Handy — but not always smart. Here's the simple version.

## What is the KOR?

The KOR is a **VAT exemption** for businesses with small revenue. If you join, you:

- charge **no VAT** to your clients,
- file **no VAT return**,
- but also **cannot reclaim input VAT** (the VAT on your costs).

## The limit: € 20,000 revenue

You may join if your revenue stays **below € 20,000 per year**. Go above it, and the KOR stops from that moment and you charge VAT again. Your revenue before that stays exempt.

## When is the KOR smart?

The KOR fits well when:

- you mostly sell to **private customers** (they can't reclaim VAT anyway, so your price is lower for them), and
- you have **few business costs** (so you miss little input VAT).

## When is it not?

The KOR is often less smart when:

- you mostly sell to **businesses** (they don't mind VAT, because they reclaim it), or
- you make **many costs with VAT** (because you then can't reclaim that VAT). Think of a start with big investments.

## What goes on your invoice?

If you join the KOR, you put **no VAT** on your invoice. You add a short note that you are exempt, for example: "VAT-exempt under the KOR scheme". More on invoices in the [invoicing guide](/en/blog/dutch-invoicing-guide).

## How do you register?

You register with the Belastingdienst. Note: if you join, you are generally committed for a few years. So think carefully about whether it suits you — or ask your bookkeeper.
